# writing-samples

Things you actually wrote. Not descriptions of how you write — `voice.md` does that — but
the real text, in full, so an agent asked to sound like you has something to read.

Without this folder, a skill asked to match your voice finds a summary of your writing
instead of your writing. That is the difference between "direct and warm, short sentences"
and a paragraph you sent a customer last month.

## What belongs here

One file per sample, named for what it is: `slack-update-pipeline-review.md`,
`spec-lifecycle-emails.md`, `linkedin-from-sewing-to-software.md`. Paste the full text,
and put a line at the top saying what it is and roughly when you wrote it.

**Short pieces are worth more than long ones**, and a spread beats a masterpiece. Three
samples across the registers you actually write in — a Slack message, a spec or a brief,
a post or an email — teach an agent far more than one polished essay, which will pull
every draft toward the personal-essay voice whether you wanted it there or not.

Include the ones you would not frame. A rushed Slack update is closer to how you really
write than anything you edited four times.

## What doesn't belong here

- **Writing that isn't yours.** A colleague's post, a competitor's page, an article you
  liked. Those may be useful, but not as a specimen of your voice.
- **Anything confidential** you would not want an agent quoting back into a draft.
- **Your style rules.** Those go in `voice.md`. This folder holds specimens; that file
  holds the guidance.

## Why they live in files and not in voice.md

`voice.md` loads on every content skill run. A full post pasted inside it would be read,
and paid for, every single time you asked for a draft. Keeping the samples out here means
a skill can reach for one when it is actually matching your voice, and skip them the rest
of the time. You manage that quota.
